What opens, and what it costs you

The container decides which path your file takes: returned untouched, repackaged in half a second, or re-encoded at full cost. This is the honest matrix.

Output is always the same: H.264 video with AAC audio inside MP4, which is what Discord embeds and plays on desktop, web and mobile.

Two different costs hiding in one button

A file that already fits never enters the encoder. MP4 comes back byte-identical under its own name; MKV or MOV gets its compressed streams copied into a new MP4 wrapper, which is a packaging exercise, not a quality decision. Measured on the live page: 0.5 seconds for a 7.96 MB MKV, versus tens of seconds for the same file re-encoded.

Everything else is a real encode: decode every frame, re-compress at a bitrate derived from your target size, and mux. That is where the seconds go, and where quality is decided.

Why AVI is refused

The browser stack this page relies on (WebCodecs plus a JavaScript demuxer) has no AVI container parser. Rather than accept the file and fail minutes later, the page rejects it at intake and names the containers that do work. If you need AVI, convert the container first — the video inside is often H.264 already, and a remux will be lossless and fast.

Why iPhone and iPad are refused

Safari on iOS exposes no video encoder to the page. The tool detects the missing API at load and says so, instead of playing the file, spinning a progress bar and failing. Android Chrome and desktop Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera and Firefox builds with WebCodecs all work.

What the target size does to the picture

Two levers move together: the bitrate budget that follows from the target size, and the resolution ladder. The page keeps the largest resolution whose bits-per-pixel stays above a floor, so a small cap lowers the resolution rather than turning the frame into mush. If a result looks worse than you expected, raise the cap before you blame the codec.
